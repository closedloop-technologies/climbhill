# Smoke Test

Test the promise before building the machinery.

## Goal

Learn whether the target user will take a meaningful action in response to the
proposed value before investing in the complete product or system.

## Flow

1. State the demand or behavior hypothesis.
2. Prototype the promise at the fidelity needed for someone to understand it.
3. Use Experiment to choose the cheapest credible exposure: landing page, fake
   door, waitlist, outreach, pricing probe, concierge offer, or similar test.
4. Precommit the action that counts as evidence. Prefer costly behavior over
   compliments or stated interest.
5. Run the test inside explicit time, cost, safety, and exposure bounds.
6. Record observations without rationalizing weak results.
7. Use Evidence to classify the hypothesis as supported, contradicted, or
   unresolved and identify the next uncertainty.

A smoke test is not successful because people say the idea is good. It is useful
when observed behavior changes what you decide to build or whether you build at
all.
