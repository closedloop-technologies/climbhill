# Experiment

Design the cheapest credible encounter with reality that can resolve an uncertain
assumption.

## Rule

Do not spend tokens answering a question that reality can answer cheaply.

## Use when

Use Experiment when an important decision depends on an empirical claim: whether
people will act, whether a system will perform, whether an intervention changes
an outcome, whether a workflow is usable, or whether an assumption survives
contact with reality.

Do not use an experiment merely to create activity. If trustworthy existing
evidence can resolve the question, research it instead.

## Procedure

1. State the decision the experiment is meant to inform.
2. State the hypothesis in falsifiable terms.
3. State at least one credible competing explanation or null outcome.
4. Identify the observable evidence that would materially change the decision.
5. Precommit thresholds or decision rules where practical.
6. Choose the smallest intervention capable of producing that evidence.
7. Prefer behavior over stated preference and direct measurement over proxy
   metrics.
8. Bound cost, time, exposure, and safety before running it.
9. Record the observations without interpreting them away.
10. Hand the result to Evidence for interpretation.

## Experiment shape

```markdown
## Decision
<what decision this evidence will inform>

## Hypothesis
<what must be true>

## Competing explanation
<null or alternative explanation>

## Intervention
<smallest credible thing we will do>

## Evidence
<what we will observe or measure>

## Decision rule
<what outcomes would support, contradict, or leave the hypothesis unresolved>

## Bounds
<time, money, sample/exposure, safety, authorization>
```

## Cheap experiments

Valid experiments include benchmarks, controlled tests, landing pages, fake
doors, concierge or Wizard-of-Oz fulfillment, pricing probes, limited outreach,
A/B tests, instrumented prototypes, hardware trials, and production canaries.
The technique is secondary to whether the experiment can change the decision.
