# On the Fragility of Self-Improving Agents

## Why this matters to ClimbHill

The most important paper for ClimbHill's evaluation contract. Apparent improvement can be caused by run variance, task order, implicit curricula, or underspecified feedback. A single better run proves almost nothing.

## What the paper establishes

The paper re-evaluates memory-based self-improving agents with repeated runs and randomized task order. It reports that adding a self-improvement loop often increases variance, and that gains seen under a default task ordering can reverse when tasks are shuffled. The authors trace part of the problem to task and environment underspecification: agents can learn plausible but unusable lessons when the feedback channel omits operational constraints or the task's actual rubric. Adding environment feedback and clearer rubrics helps, but does not eliminate the instability.

## ClimbHill design consequences

- Require repeated evaluations for claims of improvement.
- Randomize or explicitly control task ordering; never let an accidental curriculum masquerade as progress.
- Freeze evaluation sets, rubric versions, environment snapshots, and evaluator configuration.
- Record dispersion and uncertainty, not only means or best scores.
- Treat evaluator and environment specification as part of the evidence contract.
- Do not promote an Attempt from one favorable run.
