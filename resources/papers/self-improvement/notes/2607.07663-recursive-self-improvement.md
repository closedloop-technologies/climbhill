# Recursive Self-Improvement in AI

## Why this matters to ClimbHill

This survey supplies the taxonomy ClimbHill needs: worker improvement, training/policy improvement, evaluator improvement, and autonomous research are different loops with different verification and governance requirements.

## What the paper establishes

The survey organizes a large recent RSI literature along two axes: what component is being improved and how closed the improvement loop is. It distinguishes bounded refinement against an external evaluator from open-ended recursive improvement in which the machinery or criteria of improvement can themselves change. A central argument is that every self-improvement claim depends on an evaluator, and stronger demonstrated improvement tends to coincide with stronger external verification signals. Self-evaluation therefore becomes a load-bearing system component rather than a secondary implementation detail.

## ClimbHill design consequences

Every Run should make explicit:

- the Focus or component allowed to change;
- the evaluator that defines better;
- the mutation surface;
- the authority envelope;
- the validation evidence required;
- who may promote the result.

Evaluator improvement must be treated as an Alignment Focus with an evaluation surface outside the evaluator being changed.

## Design question

Which combinations of Focus and loop closure should ClimbHill permit automatically, and which should always require a new human Decision or Authorization Envelope?
