# Zetta

## Why this matters to ClimbHill

Zetta is the strongest model for separating improvement across timescales: immediate runtime recovery, rollout-level diagnosis, and validation-gated changes to reusable skills.

## What the paper establishes

Zetta keeps the base robot policy frozen while evolving code-based critics and recovery skills around it. Its architecture separates three loops: fast critic-governed recovery during execution, rollout-batch diagnosis and candidate generation after failures, and a slower validation gate before reusable skills enter the persistent harness. Candidate changes are tested through shadow replay, same-seed comparison, and held-out evaluation before promotion. This makes the distinction between temporary recovery and durable learning operational rather than rhetorical.

## ClimbHill design consequences

- Runtime recovery may affect the current Attempt without mutating durable policy.
- Continuation Analysis should happen after evidence reaches a stable checkpoint.
- Candidate reusable improvements should be versioned and tested outside the evidence used to create them.
- Promotion into future Runs requires a stricter gate than continuing the current Run.
- Keep proposal, execution, validation, and authority as separate roles.

## Design question

Should ClimbHill formalize separate continuation policies for within-Attempt recovery, between-Attempt search, and cross-Run promotion, or keep one policy with explicit authority boundaries for each timescale?
