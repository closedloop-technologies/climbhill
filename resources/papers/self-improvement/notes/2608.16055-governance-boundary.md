# Governance at the Boundary

## Why this matters to ClimbHill

The paper directly tests a failure mode that matters once ClimbHill delegates across agents: policy-relevant facts can disappear at component handoffs even when they were discovered correctly upstream.

## What the paper establishes

Using a benchmark focused on governed financial-agent behavior, the authors compare a single-loop agent with decomposed pipeline and orchestrator/subagent architectures. They find that decomposition can attenuate policy facts before those facts reach the component responsible for acting on them. The same mechanism can cause both under-escalation and over-escalation depending on whether the lost information was a risk signal or an exculpating fact. The effect is reduced with a stronger model but does not disappear as an architectural concern.

## ClimbHill design consequences

- Pass typed obligations and evidence references across boundaries, not lossy prose summaries alone.
- Keep Authorization Envelope constraints machine-readable and monotonic through delegation.
- Require delegated work to return provenance for policy-relevant observations.
- Verify that obligations survive every handoff before an action becomes eligible.
- Audit both missing constraints and missing exculpating evidence.

## Design question

Should ClimbHill make an explicit `obligations` field part of every delegated execution context, with deterministic checks that returned Continuation Analysis cannot omit or weaken inherited obligations?
