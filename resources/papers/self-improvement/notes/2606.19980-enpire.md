# ENPIRE

## Why this matters to ClimbHill

The clearest end-to-end example of a reset → execute → verify → diagnose → modify → rerun improvement loop, with experiment branches and preserved evidence.

## What the paper establishes

ENPIRE turns real-world robot policy improvement into an agent-operable experimental system. The environment layer provides automated reset, safety constraints, and outcome verification; agents then propose policy or training changes, run physical rollouts, inspect logs and video, and iterate. The framework also scales experiments across multiple robots and coding agents, making independent hypothesis exploration and branch-based collaboration explicit. The key systems insight is that autonomous improvement only becomes practical after the environment is made repeatable and objectively verifiable.

## ClimbHill design consequences

- Model Attempts as immutable experimental branches with lineage.
- Make reset/recovery and objective verification prerequisites for unattended continuation.
- Capture configuration, code revision, execution evidence, evaluator results, cost, and environment identity together.
- Keep experiment execution separate from the authority to accept or promote a result.
- Make parallel exploration mergeable only through explicit Decisions.

## Design question

What is ClimbHill's equivalent of ENPIRE's environment contract for software repositories: the minimum reset, isolation, observability, and verification capabilities required before an autonomous Run may continue?
